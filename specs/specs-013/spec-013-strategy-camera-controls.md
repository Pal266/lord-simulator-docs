# Title

Strategy Camera Controls

# Type

feature

# Description

Replace the loaded-plot free camera's awkward navigation with a faster, Manor Lords-inspired strategy-camera control scheme. The player must be able to pan rapidly with WASD or by moving the pointer to a viewport edge, rotate horizontally with Q/E, freely orbit and tilt by holding the middle mouse button, and zoom with the mouse wheel. Camera navigation must remain compatible with the existing plot-loading, region-selection, and menu input lifecycle. This is a camera-control change, not a new gameplay or map-streaming system.

# Requirements

1. **Existing camera and scope.** Modify the existing non-colliding `ALordSimulatorRegionCamera` (`Source/LordSimulator/Private/Region/LordSimulatorRegionCamera.cpp` and its header) and only the supporting region/UI integration needed for these controls. Keep the camera independent of a player pawn and preserve the existing loaded-plot camera spawn behavior: centre of the selected 10 × 10 km landscape (`X=500000`, `Y=500000` cm), 500 m above the landscape's measured centre height, facing north (`Yaw=-90°`) at `Pitch=-45°`, with zero roll. Continue to use the `ULordSimulatorRegionSubsystem` as owner of the loaded plot and camera. When the region overview is dismissed or the current plot is reselected, preserve the full existing camera view; when a different plot is successfully selected, initialize the view over the new plot using these defaults. Do not create a new camera Blueprint, camera art, character controller, gameplay pawn, map content, third-party dependency, or persistent setting.

2. **Camera focus/orbit model.** Use a single world-space focus point and camera-to-focus distance for navigation. On initial plot entry, derive the focus point by intersecting the initial camera's forward ray with the horizontal plane at the measured landscape centre height; the initial distance is therefore `500 / sin(45°) ≈ 707.1068 m`. Position the camera at `Focus - Forward(Yaw, Pitch) × Distance` after camera controls update; zero roll. Keyboard yaw and middle-button drag orbit around this focus rather than translating or rolling the scene, preserving the focus point and distance. The focus point and camera actor are transient rendering/navigation state, not world-simulation state. This focus model supersedes in-place free-camera yaw/pitch changes only for the controls defined here.

3. **WASD panning and increased speed.** `W`, `A`, `S`, and `D` pan both the camera and its focus point horizontally along the camera's **yaw-only** forward/right vectors, so movement follows the current compass heading without gaining or losing altitude from pitch. Use a speed in metres per second of `clamp(300 × (FocusDistanceMetres / 707.1068), 90, 1500)`. The initial movement speed at the established starting view is therefore `300 m/s`, three times the previous `100 m/s`; nearer views move more precisely and farther views move faster. Use elapsed time in seconds, not movement per frame. Normalize the combined direction so diagonal movement is no faster than a single cardinal direction; opposing directions cancel. No acceleration, movement-speed hotkeys, or Shift-based speed modifier is introduced.

4. **Keyboard rotation.** While the plot camera can accept gameplay input, holding `Q` rotates the camera view to the left and holding `E` rotates it to the right, by changing yaw at a constant `90°/second`, including across the ±180° wrap. `Q` and `E` MUST NOT move the camera vertically or change pitch, focus point, zoom distance, or roll. Simultaneously pressing both keys cancels keyboard yaw input. Keyboard rotation and panning use the same elapsed-time-based update and are independent of frame rate.

5. **Middle mouse drag rotation and tilt.** Pressing and holding the **middle mouse button** (clicking the scroll wheel, `MiddleMouseButton`) and moving the pointer rotates the camera around its focus: horizontal movement changes yaw at `0.20°` per physical mouse pixel, and vertical movement changes pitch at `0.20°` per physical mouse pixel. A drag toward the right turns the view right; dragging **up** tilts the view toward the horizon (pitch increases toward zero), and dragging **down** tilts toward an overhead view (pitch becomes more negative). Clamp pitch to `[-85°, -15°]`; yaw rotates freely through 360° without a stop; roll remains zero. Use **raw relative mouse displacement**, not frame-time-multiplied pixel displacement, and apply only displacement received while MMB is held. Capture relative pointer motion during the hold to permit continuous dragging at window edges; release capture and restore normal pointer visibility/location on mouse-button release, viewport focus loss, menu activation, camera teardown, or application window deactivation. Do not let release/reacquisition produce a sudden rotation jump. Neither a middle-button click without movement nor a right-button drag changes camera orientation. Remove the existing right-mouse-button camera-rotation behavior; the right mouse button remains free for future contextual world actions, without adding any such actions now.

6. **Mouse-wheel zoom.** Replace the existing mouse-wheel movement-speed multiplier with actual strategy-camera zoom. Each wheel-up notch divides the focus distance by `1.20` (zoom in); each wheel-down notch multiplies it by `1.20` (zoom out). Clamp focus distance to `[150 m, 3500 m]`; if a notch would exceed an endpoint, use the endpoint exactly. Recompute the camera position along its viewing direction relative to the same focus point; preserve yaw, pitch and focus point. Wheel input MUST NOT change a stored movement-speed multiplier, directly change camera pitch, or open a UI. Zoom is towards the view-centre focus, **not** zoom-to-pointer; no camera-zoom options UI is introduced. The terrain is not modified, and the camera remains non-colliding as in the established rendering-test camera. The speed defined in Requirement 3 scales automatically with the new focus distance.

7. **Viewport-edge scrolling.** When a plot is active and its gameplay viewport has pointer focus, moving the pointer into a `16`-physical-pixel band along the **inside** of the viewport's left, right, top, or bottom border must continuously pan the map in the corresponding on-screen direction: left = `A`, right = `D`, top = `W`, bottom = `S`. Pan begins upon entering the band and stops immediately upon leaving it; no mouse click is required. Corners combine the two adjacent directions, normalized to the same speed as single-axis panning. Use the exact same yaw-relative horizontal movement and zoom-scaled speed as WASD. Evaluate borders against the **game viewport client area**, not the desktop/monitor edges; the feature must work in fullscreen, windowed, and ultrawide modes. The pointer must be **inside** the viewport to trigger edge scrolling. Do not trigger it while the mouse is captured for MMB rotation, while the viewport is unfocused/inactive, while the pointer is outside the client area, or while a gameplay-blocking Menu/Modal screen is active. Edge scrolling and WASD directions combine and normalize once, rather than producing double speed on the same side. Do not synthesize world clicks, screen drag gestures, or menu navigation from edge scrolling.

8. **Loaded-plot navigation bounds.** The current prototype loads only one `10 × 10 km` rendering tile at a time. Clamp the **focus point's horizontal local coordinates** to `X ∈ [0, 1000000] cm` and `Y ∈ [0, 1000000] cm` for the current tile after every pan, without altering its Z coordinate. At a boundary, discard the out-of-bounds part of the pan but allow movement tangential to that boundary. This bound applies equally to WASD and edge scrolling; it does not trigger an automatic tile switch or preload neighbouring levels. Do not clamp the actual camera actor's X/Y position independently: orbiting or zooming must remain geometrically consistent with the focus point and viewing direction. Keep the existing explicit region-overview mechanism as the only way to select another plot. Do not add terrain-following, collision-avoidance, automatic cross-tile scrolling, or a new region-streaming system.

9. **Input priority and lifecycle.** The controls in this specification operate only if the current plot has successfully loaded and the existing region/UI input gate permits camera commands (`ULordSimulatorRegionSubsystem::CanMoveCamera()` or its behaviorally equivalent successor). Block translation, Q/E rotation, MMB drag, edge scrolling, and wheel zoom during a region-selection overview, active Menu/Modal screen, asynchronous plot transition, and after leaving the plot for the main menu. Preserve the application's established `M`/`Escape` UI navigation and input priority without assigning either key to a new camera function; neither opening nor closing a screen should trigger camera motion. Releasing a held key or button during blocked input must not cause stale motion when the screen closes. Do not alter global time simulation, game/UI input ownership, menu focus, plot-selection semantics, or application shutdown behavior.

10. **No new content or settings.** All behavior uses C++ and existing engine input/viewport facilities. Add no camera UI controls, tutorial text, tooltips, localization keys, production textures, approved visual assets, sounds, animations, player-facing error messages, Lord Simulator project log messages, persistent settings keys, rebinding UI, or new external libraries. Runtime parameters are the exact fixed values in this specification; do not invent additional adjustable speeds or change the existing localization and settings lifecycles.

11. **Automated and packaged verification.** Update existing camera-related Unreal Automation tests under `Source/LordSimulatorTests/Private/RegionNavigationTests.cpp` (and add focused test coverage if helpful) to reflect the new bindings and no longer assert superseded `Q/E` vertical translation, RMB rotation, or wheel-adjusted movement speed. Deterministically verify initial transform/focus, time-scaled and yaw-relative WASD motion, normalization and cancellation, increased/zoom-dependent speed, `Q/E` directions and frame-rate invariance, MMB yaw/pitch sensitivity and limits, MMB release/focus-loss cleanup, right-button non-rotation, zoom direction and limits, edge-band detection/direction/corners/viewport-exit behavior, map-focus bounds, and blocking by the existing UI gate. Use deterministic input seams for pointer coordinates, relative movement, and viewport size where necessary instead of relying on real OS pointer timing. Preserve existing plot-load, reselect, dismissal, and transition regression tests. Validate manually in an actual viewport at `1280 × 720`, `1920 × 1080`, and `3440 × 1440`, in windowed and fullscreen modes: verify perceptibly faster default WASD, MMB yaw/tilt, Q/E yaw, wheel zoom, four-edge/corner pan, no unintended UI input, and correct cleanup across menu opening and plot changes. Build the established Win64 Development Editor and Win64 Shipping targets and perform packaged-game verification. Update the game repository's `README.md` camera-control instructions to match verified behavior; do not change unrelated build or content-generation workflows.

12. **Project-standard update.** Update `standards/PROJECT_STANDARD.md` in the documentation repository to record the implemented loaded-plot camera input contract and the replacement of the previous Q/E vertical motion, right-button rotation and wheel speed changes with keyboard yaw, MMB orbit/tilt, wheel zoom and viewport-edge panning. Record the defined tile-focus navigation boundary and confirm the camera remains independent of simulation identity. Preserve unrelated project-standard requirements, including current M/Escape menu navigation and CommonUI input gating. Do not consider this feature complete until the code, tests, README and project standard agree.

# Acceptance Criteria

1. A newly entered plot retains its established initial camera view, with an orbit focus derived from the stated plane intersection; reselecting the same plot or dismissing the region overview preserves the complete view, while a newly entered different plot gets the default view.
2. WASD pans relative to current yaw at `300 m/s` in the initial view, scales with focus distance as specified, is elapsed-time based, and never gains diagonal speed; opposing inputs cancel.
3. Q rotates left and E rotates right at `90°/second`, including yaw wrap and simultaneous-key cancellation, without vertical motion or pitch change.
4. Holding MMB permits continuous yaw/pitch orbit at `0.20°/pixel`, with the specified drag directions and `[-85°, -15°]` pitch limits. Mouse capture is cleaned up on release/focus loss/UI change, and RMB no longer rotates the camera.
5. The wheel zooms by the specified `1.20` ratio between `150 m` and `3500 m`, maintaining the focus and current orientation; the previous wheel-based movement-speed adjustment is gone.
6. Pointer placement within `16` physical pixels of each viewport edge pans in that direction; corners and simultaneous WASD normalize correctly. Edge panning stops when the pointer exits the band/viewport, MMB is active, focus is lost, or gameplay input is blocked; it works in windowed and fullscreen viewports.
7. Panning cannot move the focus beyond the loaded tile's `0–1000000 cm` X/Y bounds, tangential panning still works at an edge, and no unrequested neighbouring plot is loaded.
8. Region selection, plot transitions, active game menus/modals, returning to main menu, and established M/Escape navigation work without camera input bleed-through or residual mouse capture. No simulation, localization, settings, visual-content, or other unrelated feature is introduced.
9. Updated automated tests pass alongside the existing tests, Development Editor and Shipping builds succeed, and the specified manual viewport and packaged-game checks pass. Camera controls are documented accurately in the game README.
10. `standards/PROJECT_STANDARD.md` describes the verified resulting camera behavior without contradicting the implementation or unrelated project standards.
